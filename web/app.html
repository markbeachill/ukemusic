The song's own tab, in steps

Tab written in the song is always played as written; the pattern is for the chords. Learn the tab in layers: the chords under it, then the picking on open strings, then both.

How picking works

Picking to sing along to. Your left hand holds the chords just as it does to strum. Your right hand picks the strings one (or two) at a time, in a pattern, instead of brushing across them all. The strip at the top of karaoke shows each string as it comes.

This is accompaniment: the tune is your voice's job, just as when you strum. A picked solo (a "chord melody", with the tune on the top strings) is different, and needs the tune written out: the Chord melody pattern makes one when the song has its tune written in.

Singing over it. Get the pattern going on its own first, then add the words; the easier patterns leave more of you free to sing. Picking is quieter than strumming, so find your first note before you start: when a song has one, it's sounded with the first click of the count-in.

The fingers. On a ukulele each finger has its own string: the thumb (p) plays g, the index finger (i) C, the middle finger (m) E and the ring finger (a) A. On a guitar the thumb takes the chord's bass note and the fingers the top three strings.

The patterns, easiest first: 1 One string a beat (g C E A); 2 Up, twice a bar; 3 Up and back (g C E A E C, lovely in 3/4 and 6/8); 4 Pinch and roll (g+A C E C); 5 Alternating thumb, or Travis (the thumb on every beat, swapping g and C).

Tab and chord boxes. Tab is the usual way to write picking: one line per string (the top line is A), and a number says which fret to hold as you pick that string (0 is open). With picked chords, hold the whole chord shape: every number in the tab is already under a finger. So read the chord, and let the tab tell your right hand which strings and when.

The pick sheet sets the song out like a fingerstyle songbook: the pattern counted out, the chord boxes, then each line as tab with the chord over the bar where it starts and the words underneath.

The tune. A song can carry its tune on a line above the words, by note name in the written key: {melody: E E F G | G F E D | C C D E | E. D/2 D2}. E2 is two beats, E/2 half a beat, E. a beat and a half, r a rest; each note is the nearest to the one before (' up an octave, , down). Lines with the same chords share the tune. Then Play the tune plays it quietly over the chords, and Chord melody makes a picked solo from it.

Changing the pattern part-way. A song can change pattern with a line like {pick: travis} or its own pattern, {pick: g C E A E C} (by string) or {pick: p i m a} (by finger); {pick: strum} goes back to strumming. Write them in the desktop app (Pick → Change here).

Learning it. Hold one chord and play the pattern until your picking hand does it on its own. Then change chords on beat 1. Then the song, slowly, a little faster each day.
