# How Learn works

The **Learn** tab helps you get a song into your head and your fingers, so you can play it without the sheet. It's in the desktop app and in the web player (**Learn**, beside Karaoke). Here's what it does, in plain words.

## What it works out from your song

- **The key.** It uses the key you set in Info → Written in. If you haven't set one, it makes a guess from the chords, the chord the song starts and ends on, and the chord most of its parts start on.
- **The chords as numbers.** In C, C is I (home), F is IV (away), G is V (wants to go home), Am is vi (the sad home). A song in numbers is the same in every key, so once you know it as "one, five, six, four" you know it anywhere.
- **What each chord does.** "Home", "tension", "leads to five", or for a chord from outside the key, where it's borrowed from or where it's heading.
- **The parts.** Parts with the same chords get the same letter, and the words tell a verse from a chorus. So you see how little there really is to learn: "9 parts, but only 4 chord patterns".
- **Familiar patterns.** Four-chord pop, the 50s progression, the three-chord trick and others, marked where they happen. Once you know one, you'll spot it in other songs.
- **The circle of fifths.** It's turned so the song's home chord is at the top. You see the chords it uses and how it moves.
- **The hardest changes.** It works out what each finger does going from one chord to the next, so you know which changes to practise first.

## How it helps you practise

- **From memory:** karaoke where the chords fade: first shown, then as numbers, then gone. Remembering something fixes it far better than reading it again.
- **Next chord?:** a quick quiz on each change in the song. You can hear each answer before you choose.
- **Trouble:** the chords you looked at, the answers you missed and the lines you marked, ready to loop slowly.
- **Pick** (its own tab): pick a strummed song instead: the chords stay, the strum becomes one of five standard picking patterns, from one string a beat to Travis picking, with a pick sheet laid out like a fingerstyle songbook. It's picking to sing along to: the tune is still your voice's job, and the song's first note (Info → First note) is sounded in the count-in. And for songs with their own tab, **Tab in steps**: When the tab sits on chord shapes, it says which, bar by bar ("Hold C, pick g-C-E-A"); a tune picked note by note is left as a tune. You learn it in layers: the chords strummed, then the picking on open strings, then both, slowly and then in time. From memory fades the tab like the chords.
- **Learn sheet:** one page (PDF and web page) with all of the above, to keep on your music stand.
- **Come back to it.** Playing a song again after a day, then after a week, keeps it far better than one long session.

## Can you trust it?

The music theory is standard: the numbers, what the chords do and the circle of fifths. So is the learning advice: practise from memory, in chunks, spread out over days.

Some parts are rules of thumb we've written and checked by hand on a handful of songs. These are guessing the key, finding and naming the parts, and ranking the changes. They work well on most songs but can be fooled by unusual ones. If the key looks wrong, set it in **Info → Written in** and everything follows.
