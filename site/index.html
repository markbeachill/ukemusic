How it works

Bring it in once. ukemusic does the rest.

Paste a chord sheet from a website, import ChordPro files, or type it. However it comes in, a song is a plain text file you can read and change anywhere, with each chord in square brackets just before the word where it changes. Everything else follows from that: the sheet, the shapes, the playback, the learning and the printing. All the ways to bring your songs, and an AI prompt for difficult sheets.

What you type 
{D-DU-UDU}
[[Verse 1]]
I [C]come from Alabama
with my [G7]banjo on my [C]knee

[[Chorus]]
Oh! Su[F]sanna,
oh [C]don't you cry for [G7]me
What you get

A closer look

From the first strum to the gig

Everything a ukulele player needs around a song, in one place.

The Session screen, for playing together

For a club night or a jam: the whole song on one screen, on the TV or projector, as big as it will go. ukemusic lays it out for you, so nobody has to scroll.

  • Verses and choruses arranged in columns, never split, with the chord shapes down the side
  • A setlist goes song by song: arrow keys, a presentation clicker or a tap for the next
  • With a TV plugged in, the song goes full screen there while the laptop shows what's next
  • Check the whole library fits one screen before the night

Play it, and play along

Press Play and hear the song on a real ukulele, strummed the way you wrote it. The sheet follows the words, with the chord now, the next shapes and the strum lit up above.

  • Slow it down, loop the part you're learning, or speed up a little each time round
  • Waltzes, swing and shuffle feel, picked out from the strum
  • Tap the tempo, or count yourself in

Karaoke for the whole room

Full screen, big words that light up as you sing, with the chord above each word and the shapes down the side. Tap any line to start from there.

Stage copies you can read at a glance

Print the song sheet for practice and a Stage copy for the gig: big, bold and easy to scan, in the format that suits you.

  • Clear, Brackets, Compact or Heavy
  • Choruses printed every time, or once and then "as before"
  • PDF, web page, words only, Markdown or ChordPro; a songbook from a setlist

Free Practice, no song needed

Warm-ups, chord changes, strumming, fingerpicking, scales and noodling, each with a moving fretboard and a metronome, plus your progress and daily routines.

Your songs, in libraries

Songs live in ordinary folders on your computer. Keep several libraries, search by artist, tag or key, put songs into setlists, and share a library as a single pack file.

ukemusic comes with a library of public-domain songs to start you off.

Learn a song by heart

The Learn tab gets a song into your head and your fingers, so you can play it without the sheet. It works out the song's key, its chords as numbers and what each one does, its parts and the familiar patterns in it, then helps you practise the way memory works best.

  • Play from memory, with the chords fading: shown, then as numbers, then gone
  • Next chord?: a quick quiz on each change, with every answer to hear
  • The hardest changes, with what each finger does, and your trouble spots to loop
  • The circle of fifths, turned so the song's home is at the top
  • A one-page learn sheet to keep on your music stand

More about learning with ukemusic · How Learn works, and how far to trust it

Fingerpick it, with the tune

The Pick tab turns a strummed song into a picked one. The chords stay as they are: choose a standard pattern and ukemusic plays it, shows each string as it's picked, and lays the song out as a pick sheet, with tab over the bars and the words underneath.

  • Five standard patterns, from one string a beat to alternating thumb, in 4/4, 3/4 and 6/8
  • Change the pattern as the song goes, or write a line or the whole song out as tab to edit note by note
  • Write the tune in by note name and see it as notes on a staff, with the chords above
  • Hear the tune quietly over the picking while you learn to sing it, or make it a chord melody solo
  • The first note to sing, sounded with the count-in

A player for your phone and tablet

The web player runs in any browser, with nothing to install: open a song, change the key, capo or instrument, play along, sing with karaoke, learn it by heart, put it on the big screen with the Session screen, and practise. It comes with the public-domain songs, so you can try it straight away. On iPhone and iPad, add it to your Home Screen.

Try the web player

It plays songs rather than writing them. Write and edit in the desktop app, then take your songs with you: its Songs for the web… saves them in one file, and Update songs in the player loads it.

And plenty more

Any key, any capo

Move a song up or down in a click, keep it in several keys, or play it with a capo and see the shapes to finger.

Ukulele, baritone or guitar

Ukulele with high or low G, baritone ukulele and guitar, each with its own chord shapes. Left-handed diagrams too.

Paste from a website

Copy a chord sheet with the chords on their own lines, and ukemusic tidies it into the song format.

Club songbooks and ChordPro

Bring in a whole folder of ChordPro songs at once, and use the strum marks clubs write in the brackets: [C↓], [x], [G…]. Tunes written in ABC come in as the song's tune, and can go out again as notation. How ukemusic and ChordPro fit together.

Tab and melody

Write tab beside the words; it plays, moves with the key and prints neatly. Write the tune in too: it plays along and shows as notes on a staff, once at the top or over every line like a songbook.

Chord patterns

See each part's chords with its words, the progressions they use, and which parts share them. The same analysis is behind Learn.

Choose your shapes

Click a chord diagram to pick another way to play it, kept with the song, or hide the diagrams of the chords you already know.

Nothing lost

Every save keeps the earlier versions, and songs are plain text files you own.

Made to be read

A guide to writing songs is built in, and a hint line explains whatever you point at.

Download

Free for Windows, Mac and Linux

The latest version is on the releases page.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. Run the installer; if Windows SmartScreen says it doesn't recognise the app, choose More info → Run anyway.

Installer (.exe)
Mac

macOS 12 or later. Open the .dmg and drag ukemusic to Applications. The first time, right-click it and choose Open, because it isn't from the App Store.

Apple silicon (.dmg)Intel (.dmg)
Linux

Most 64-bit distributions. Download the AppImage, make it executable (right-click → Properties → Allow executing, or chmod +x) and run it.

AppImage

Build it yourself from the source

You'll need Node.js 20 or later.

git clone https://github.com/OWNER/ukemusic.git
cd ukemusic
npm install
npm start            # run ukemusic
npm test             # run the tests
npm run package      # make an installer for this computer, in dist/
