ukemusic: chord sheets, play-along and practice for the ukulele

For ukulele, baritone ukulele and guitar, left- or right-handed

Paste a chord sheet from a website, or import your club's songbook. ukemusic puts the chords into the words, shows the chord shapes and the strum, plays the song with you, helps you learn it by heart and prints it for the gig.

How a song is written

However it comes in, a song is a plain text file you can read and change anywhere, with each chord in square brackets just before the word where it changes. The sheet, the shapes, the playback, the learning and the printing all come from that. All the ways to add songs.

What you type 
{D-DU-UDU}
[[Verse 1]]
I [C]come from Alabama
with my [G7]banjo on my [C]knee

[[Chorus]]
Oh! Su[F]sanna,
oh [C]don't you cry for [G7]me
What you get 
Verse 1  C
I come from Alabama
        G7          C
with my banjo on my knee
Chorus      F
Oh! Susanna,
   C                 G7
oh don't you cry for me
  • [C]word: the chord changes on that word
  • {D-DU-UDU}: the strum (down, up, and a gap)
  • [[Chorus]]: a part of the song, by name

Everything you can type is in How to write songs.

The main features

Each is described step by step in the user guide.

Playing along

Press Play to hear the song on a real ukulele, strummed the way it's written. The sheet follows the words, with the chord now, the strum and the next shapes above it.

  • Slow it down without changing the song (Practice speed), loop the part you're learning, or speed up a little each time round
  • Waltzes, swing and shuffle feel, taken from the strum
  • Tap the tempo, or count yourself in

Karaoke

Full screen, with big words that light up as you sing, the chord above each word and the chord shapes down the side. Tap any line to start from there.

Learning a song by heart

The Learn tab works out the song's key, its chords as numbers, its parts and the familiar patterns in it, then helps you practise without the sheet.

  • Play from memory, with the chords fading: shown, then as numbers, then gone
  • Next chord?: a quiz on each change, with every answer to hear
  • The hardest changes, with what each finger does, and your trouble spots to loop
  • A one-page learn sheet to keep on your music stand

Free Practice

Forty exercises with no song needed: warm-ups, chord changes, strumming, fingerpicking, scales and making up tunes, each with a moving fretboard and a metronome. Today's practice picks three, and Getting started suggests an order for beginners.

The Music stand, for reading from a tablet

Your songs on an iPad or laptop on your music stand, instead of paper: one steady text size, a page at a time, song after song. In the web player and the desktop app. Music stand, Session screen or Stage copy: which one?

  • Tap the right half of the screen for the next page or song, the left half to go back
  • Tap the top (or ☰ Menu) to close it, jump to a song or change the text size
  • A setlist goes song by song in its keys; a Bluetooth page-turner pedal works too

The Session screen, for playing together

For a club night or a jam: the whole song on the TV or projector, as big as it will go, laid out so nobody has to scroll.

  • Verses and choruses in columns, never split, with the chord shapes down the side
  • A setlist goes song by song: the arrow keys, a presentation clicker or a tap for the next
  • With a TV plugged in, the song fills it while the laptop shows what's next

Stage copies to print

Print the song sheet for practice and a Stage copy for the gig: big, bold and easy to scan.

  • Chords above the words, in the words, or Heavy
  • Choruses printed every time, or once and then "as before"
  • PDF, web page, words only, Markdown or ChordPro; a songbook from a setlist

Fingerpicking, with the tune

The Picking tab turns a strummed song into a picked one. Choose a standard pattern and ukemusic plays it, shows each string as it's picked, and makes a pick sheet with tab over the bars and the words underneath.

  • Five standard patterns, from one string a beat to alternating thumb, in 4/4, 3/4 and 6/8
  • Write the tune in by note name and see it as notes on a staff, with the chords above
  • Hear the tune quietly over the picking while you learn to sing it

Your songs, in libraries

On the desktop, songs are ordinary folders on your computer. Show them as tiles or a list, search by artist, tag or key, put them into setlists, and keep several libraries. ukemusic comes with a library of public-domain songs to start you off.

On a phone or tablet

The web player runs in any browser with nothing to install, and once it's on your Home Screen it works offline too. Open a song, change the key, capo or instrument, play along, learn it, read it on the Music stand, and practise. Write and change songs there too.

Bring your songs in from files, or keep the desktop app's songs folder in Dropbox so both share one set of songs. Putting it on your Home Screen and keeping your songs safe.

Build it yourself from the source

You'll need Node.js 20 or later.

git clone https://github.com/OWNER/ukemusic.git
cd ukemusic
npm install
npm start            # run ukemusic
npm test             # run the tests
npm run package      # make an installer for this computer, in dist/
